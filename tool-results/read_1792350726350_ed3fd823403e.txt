  1572→2.5 Automatic Differentiation
  1573→Recall from Section 2.4 that calculating derivatives is the crucial step in all the optimization
  1574→algorithms that we will use to train deep networks. While the calculations are straightfor-
  1575→ward, working them out by hand can be tedious and error-prone, and these issues only grow
  1576→as our models become more complex.
  1577→Fortunately all modern deep learning frameworks take this work off our plates by offering
  1578→automatic differentiation (often shortened to autograd). As we pass data through each
  1579→successive function, the framework builds a computational graph that tracks how each value
  1580→depends on others. To calculate derivatives, automatic differentiation works backwards
  1581→through this graph applying the chain rule. The computational algorithm for applying the
  1582→chain rule in this fashion is called backpropagation.
  1583→While autograd libraries have become a hot concern over the past decade, they have a
  1584→long history. In fact the earliest references to autograd date back over half of a century
  1585→(Wengert, 1964). The core ideas behind modern backpropagation date to a PhD thesis
  1586→from 1980 (Speelpenning, 1980) and were further developed in the late 1980s (Griewank,
  1587→1989). While backpropagation has become the default method for computing gradients,
  1588→it is not the only option. For instance, the Julia programming language employs forward
  1589→propagation (Revels et al., 2016). Before exploring methods, let’s first master the autograd
  1590→package.

  1778→2.6 Probability and Statistics
  1779→One way or another, machine learning is all about uncertainty. In supervised learning, we
  1780→want to predict something unknown (the target) given something known (the features). De-
  1781→pending on our objective, we might attempt to predict the most likely value of the target.
  1782→Or we might predict the value with the smallest expected distance from the target. And
  1783→sometimes we wish not only to predict a specific value but to quantify our uncertainty. For
  1784→example, given some features describing a patient, we might want to know how likely they
  1785→are to suffer a heart attack in the next year. In unsupervised learning, we often care about
  1786→uncertainty. To determine whether a set of measurements are anomalous, it helps to know
  1787→how likely one is to observe values in a population of interest. Furthermore, in reinforce-
  1788→ment learning, we wish to develop agents that act intelligently in various environments.
  1789→This requires reasoning about how an environment might be expected to change and what
  1790→rewards one might expect to encounter in response to each of the available actions.
  1791→Probability is the mathematical field concerned with reasoning under uncertainty. Given a
  1792→probabilistic model of some process, we can reason about the likelihood of various events.
  1793→The use of probabilities to describe the frequencies of repeatable events (like coin tosses) is
  1794→fairly uncontroversial. In fact, frequentist scholars adhere to an interpretation of probability
  1795→that applies only to such repeatable events. By contrast Bayesian scholars use the language
  1796→of probability more broadly to formalize reasoning under uncertainty. Bayesian probability
  1797→is characterized by two unique features: (i) assigning degrees of belief to non-repeatable
  1798→events, e.g., what is the probability that a dam will collapse?; and (ii) subjectivity. While
  1799→Bayesian probability provides unambiguous rules for how one should update their beliefs in
  1800→light of new evidence, it allows for different individuals to start off with different prior be-
  1801→liefs. Statistics helps us to reason backwards, starting off with collection and organization
  1802→of data and backing out to what inferences we might draw about the process that generated
  1803→the data. Whenever we analyze a dataset, hunting for patterns that we hope might charac-
  1804→terize a broader population, we are employing statistical thinking. Many courses, majors,
  1805→theses, careers, departments, companies, and institutions have been devoted to the study of
  1806→probability and statistics. While this section only scratches the surface, we will provide the
  1807→foundation that you need to begin building models.
