   845→Biological inspiration is what gave neural networks their name. For over a century (dating

   847→have tried to assemble computational circuits that resemble networks of interacting neurons.
   848→Over time, the interpretation of biology has become less literal, but the name stuck. At its
   849→heart lie a few key principles that can be found in most networks today:
   850→• The alternation of linear and nonlinear processing units, often referred to as layers.

   852→the entire network at once.
   853→After initial rapid progress, research in neural networks languished from around 1995 until
   854→2005. This was mainly due to two reasons. First, training a network is computationally
   855→very expensive. While random-access memory was plentiful at the end of the past century,
   856→computational power was scarce. Second, datasets were relatively small. In fact, Fisher’s
   857→Iris dataset from 1936 was still a popular tool for testing the efficacy of algorithms. The
   858→MNIST dataset with its 60,000 handwritten digits was considered huge.
   859→Given the scarcity of data and computation, strong statistical tools such as kernel methods,
   860→decision trees, and graphical models proved empirically superior in many applications.
   861→Moreover, unlike neural networks, they did not require weeks to train and provided pre-
   862→dictable results with strong theoretical guarantees.

  1093→Frequently, questions about a coming AI apocalypse and the plausibility of a singularity

  1102→oriented manner. While their behavior might give the illusion of general intelligence, it is a
  1103→combination of rules, heuristics and statistical models that underlie the design. Second, at
  1104→present, there are simply no tools for artificial general intelligence that are able to improve

  1108→many routine tasks, currently fulfilled by humans, can and will be automated. Farm robots

  1117→1.7 The Essence of Deep Learning
  1118→Thus far, we have talked in broad terms about machine learning. Deep learning is the subset
  1119→of machine learning concerned with models based on many-layered neural networks. It is
  1120→deep in precisely the sense that its models learn many layers of transformations. While this
  1121→might sound narrow, deep learning has given rise to a dizzying array of models, techniques,
  1122→problem formulations, and applications. Many intuitions have been developed to explain
  1123→the benefits of depth. Arguably, all machine learning has many layers of computation, the
  1124→first consisting of feature processing steps. What differentiates deep learning is that the
  1125→operations learned at each of the many layers of representations are learned jointly from
  1126→data.

  1130→Introduction
  1131→The problems that we have discussed so far, such as learning from the raw audio signal, the
  1132→raw pixel values of images, or mapping between sentences of arbitrary lengths and their
  1133→counterparts in foreign languages, are those where deep learning excels and traditional
  1134→methods falter. It turns out that these many-layered models are capable of addressing low-
  1135→level perceptual data in a way that previous tools could not. Arguably the most significant
  1136→commonality in deep learning methods is end-to-end training. That is, rather than assem-
  1137→bling a system based on components that are individually tuned, one builds the system and
  1138→then tunes their performance jointly. For instance, in computer vision scientists used to
  1139→separate the process of feature engineering from the process of building machine learn-
  1140→ing models. The Canny edge detector (Canny, 1987) and Lowe’s SIFT feature extractor
  1141→(Lowe, 2004) reigned supreme for over a decade as algorithms for mapping images into
  1142→feature vectors. In bygone days, the crucial part of applying machine learning to these
  1143→problems consisted of coming up with manually-engineered ways of transforming the data
  1144→into some form amenable to shallow models. Unfortunately, there is only so much that
  1145→humans can accomplish by ingenuity in comparison with a consistent evaluation over mil-
  1146→lions of choices carried out automatically by an algorithm. When deep learning took over,
  1147→these feature extractors were replaced by automatically tuned filters that yielded superior
  1148→accuracy.
  1149→Thus, one key advantage of deep learning is that it replaces not only the shallow models at
  1150→the end of traditional learning pipelines, but also the labor-intensive process of feature engi-
  1151→neering. Moreover, by replacing much of the domain-specific preprocessing, deep learning
  1152→has eliminated many of the boundaries that previously separated computer vision, speech
  1153→recognition, natural language processing, medical informatics, and other application areas,
  1154→thereby offering a unified set of tools for tackling diverse problems.
  1155→Beyond end-to-end training, we are experiencing a transition from parametric statistical
  1156→descriptions to fully nonparametric models. When data is scarce, one needs to rely on sim-
  1157→plifying assumptions about reality in order to obtain useful models. When data is abundant,
  1158→these can be replaced by nonparametric models that better fit the data. To some extent, this
  1159→mirrors the progress that physics experienced in the middle of the previous century with
  1160→the availability of computers. Rather than solving by hand parametric approximations of
  1161→how electrons behave, one can now resort to numerical simulations of the associated par-
  1162→tial differential equations. This has led to much more accurate models, albeit often at the
  1163→expense of interpretation.
  1164→Another difference from previous work is the acceptance of suboptimal solutions, dealing
  1165→with nonconvex nonlinear optimization problems, and the willingness to try things before
  1166→proving them. This new-found empiricism in dealing with statistical problems, combined
  1167→with a rapid influx of talent has led to rapid progress in the development of practical algo-
  1168→rithms, albeit in many cases at the expense of modifying and re-inventing tools that existed
  1169→for decades.

  1181→1.8 Summary
  1182→Machine learning studies how computer systems can leverage experience (often data) to
  1183→improve performance at specific tasks. It combines ideas from statistics, data mining, and
  1184→optimization. Often, it is used as a means of implementing AI solutions. As a class of
  1185→machine learning, representational learning focuses on how to automatically find the ap-
  1186→propriate way to represent data. Considered as multi-level representation learning through
  1187→learning many layers of transformations, deep learning replaces not only the shallow mod-
  1188→els at the end of traditional machine learning pipelines, but also the labor-intensive process
  1189→of feature engineering. Much of the recent progress in deep learning has been triggered
  1190→by an abundance of data arising from cheap sensors and Internet-scale applications, and
  1191→by significant progress in computation, mostly through GPUs. Furthermore, the availabil-
  1192→ity of efficient deep learning frameworks has made design and implementation of whole
  1193→system optimization significantly easier, and this is a key component in obtaining high
  1194→performance.
